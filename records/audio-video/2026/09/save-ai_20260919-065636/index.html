Transcript

Using Claude Code every day to write things, looking at the API bill at the end of the month is really painful. I only realized later that the money isn't actually being spent on the model, but on that pile of useless context—every time, the AI has to re-read the entire stack of logs, files, and command outputs, most of which is useless fluff, and you're paying for every single word.
There is an open-source tool on GitHub with 49,000 stars called headroom, specifically designed for this: compressing content before sending it to a large model. In an official test during a troubleshooting case, 65,000 tokens were compressed down to just over 5,000, and the answer didn't change by a single word.
The best part is that it's practically zero-cost: a single command takes over your Claude Code, or spins up a proxy, without changing a single word of code. The original text stays on your local machine, so you can pull up the full version whenever you want, and your data never leaves your computer.
Of course, don't overhype it either. How much it saves depends on what you're compressing — structured data like logs and code can be cut by 80 to 90 percent, while pure chat is only around 20 to 30 percent. But the direction is definitely right.
